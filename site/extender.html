OpenSync extenders

The extenders ("pods") run the open-source OpenSync release in a Linux container with nothing but two simulated radios. They onboard exactly as hardware does: over the gateway's Wi-Fi backhaul, through a GRE tunnel, to the cloud.

Building the extender

./build-pod.sh all        # buildenv + sources + build + image
./build-pod.sh status
sources

OpenSync 6.6.1.0

From github.com/plume-design, pinned in pod/opensync/sources.lock: core, opensync-platform-cfg80211 (nl80211, so mac80211_hwsim), opensync-vendor-openwrt-template, the local service provider.

overlays

HWSIM_POD target

This repo's target on the vendor template (systemd, the backhaul STA list, null IGMP/MLD/UPnP backends) and the mvx-local service provider: redirector = local-noc, onboarding credentials.

toolchain

Native Ubuntu build

An Ubuntu 20.04 build container with OVS 2.8.7 and hostap d9d5e55 plus OpenSync's patches, instead of the OpenWrt SDK.

image

LXD system container

Ubuntu 20.04 + systemd, the OpenSync rootfs, ovsdb-server, hostapd/wpa_supplicant, and a runtime bootstrap that binds OpenSync's radio config to the phys the container actually gets.

Onboarding, step by step

What happens between deploy-mvx.sh pod pod-1 and a client on pod-1 reaching the internet. Nothing here is injected: the extender's own OpenSync (cm, owm, nm) and local-noc acting as the cloud do all of it.

1 · Launch

70-pod.sh starts the container with two radios from the pool as wlan0/wlan1 and no wired NIC. Before any OpenSync manager runs, mvx-pod-bootstrap writes the radio config for the phys actually present (wiphy names are VM-wide): the first radio 2.4 GHz for the fronthaul, the second 5 GHz with the backhaul STA bhaul-sta-50, carrying the onboarding credentials the image was built with.

2 · Join the backhaul

owm brings up bhaul-sta-50, which finds mv3's wl1.1 ("opensync-lab-bhaul", WPA2, channel 44) and associates. On mv3, the Wi-Fi HAL's own authenticator answers: authentication, association, the WPA2 4-way handshake.

3 · A backhaul address

The STA gets 169.254.1.x/25 from mv3's dnsmasq on wl1.1. mv3 now lists the extender among the backhaul AP's associated clients and in its DHCP leases, both reported to the cloud.

4 · The extender's end of the tunnel

cm (its backhaul GRE module) sees a link-local address on a backhaul STA and creates g-bhaul-sta-50: a gretap from 169.254.1.x to 169.254.1.1 over bhaul-sta-50, and bridges it into br-home.

5 · The gateway's end

local-noc, like the cloud, sees on mv3 an associated backhaul client with a lease and creates mv3's end: Wifi_Inet_Config pgd1_x (gre, over wl1.1, remote the extender), which mv3's nm builds, and the tunnel as an OVS port of brlan0. The extender is now on mv3's LAN.

6 · A LAN address

DHCP on br-home crosses the tunnel: the extender gets 192.168.0.x from mv3 with mv3 as its default route, and so a path to the internet through mv3's WAN.

7 · The cloud

cm connects to its redirector (tcp:10.101.0.40:6640, built into the image), is sent to the controller (:6641), and local-noc starts monitoring every table of the extender's database. The extender is claimed.

8 · The fronthaul

local-noc gives the extender its fronthaul: home-ap-24, "opensync-lab-home", WPA2, channel 6, in br-home. owm brings it up.

9 · A client on the extender

80-client.sh starts an Alpine container with one radio and no wired NIC. Its wpa_supplicant joins its extender's home-ap-24 (pinned to that BSSID: every extender uses the same SSID), udhcpc gets 192.168.0.x from mv3 through the extender's tunnel, and ICMP, DNS and HTTP reach the internet.

Seeing the GRE at work

On the extender, the tunnel and its bridge; then a client's ping inside the tunnel on the backhaul radio, and arriving at mv3:

V=opensync-lab-0923
lxc exec $V -- lxc exec pod-1 -- ip -d link show g-bhaul-sta-50
lxc exec $V -- lxc exec pod-1 -- ls /sys/class/net/br-home/brif
lxc exec $V -- lxc exec pod-1-wc1 -- ping 8.8.8.8                            # keep it running, then:
lxc exec $V -- lxc exec pod-1 -- tcpdump -nn -i bhaul-sta-50 'ip proto 47'
lxc exec $V -- lxc exec mv3 -- tcpdump -nn -i wl1.1 'ip proto 47'
# pod-1, on the backhaul radio: the client's ping, inside the GRE
169.254.1.88 > 169.254.1.1: GREv0, length 102: IP 192.168.0.143 > 8.8.8.8: ICMP echo request, id 492, seq 2
169.254.1.1 > 169.254.1.88: GREv0, length 102: IP 8.8.8.8 > 192.168.0.143: ICMP echo reply, id 492, seq 2

The topology viewer shows the same from the cloud's side: hover an extender for both ends of its tunnel and its traffic, counted by mv3's OVS port.

The wireless clients

Each client runs its networking like a device: an OpenRC boot script (/etc/local.d/opensync-lab-wlan.start) starts wpa_supplicant on its extender's fronthaul and a udhcpc that stays running, keeping and renewing the lease, also after a reassociation or a restart. The lab has no IPv6 upstream, so the clients stay on IPv4. The check proves association to the right extender, the lease from mv3, the running DHCP client, ICMP, DNS and HTTP.

What it took

OpenSync on simulated radios needed a few fixes, each in this repo and listed with its proper upstream fix in the reference:

  • No GRE without the STA list: OpenSync 6.6's cm builds an extender's tunnel only for the STAs in CONFIG_OVSDB_BOOTSTRAP_WIFI_STA_LIST; the target sets it.
  • Multi-band radios: a hwsim radio offers 2.4, 5 and 6 GHz at once; a small core patch gives each phy one band.
  • An owm that aborted every 3 minutes: with a tx_chainmask configured, osw's config sync never settles on hwsim and owm aborts after 180 s, taking the backhaul down; the bootstrap sets none.
  • The gateway's Wi-Fi HAL: it restarted the backhaul AP on every config re-apply and never forgot a station; two meta-mvx patches fix both.
